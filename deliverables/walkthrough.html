Walkthrough · 3 / 5 · edge cases
Decay is linear; caps, floors and clock-skew hold.
newton — scenarios
$ python -m analysis.scenarios          # trimmed to four groups
[decay] one offence (-5) recovers linearly over its 28d window
  score  0d after the offence    95   (penalty -5.00)
  score  7d after the offence    96   (penalty -3.75)
  score 14d after the offence    98   (penalty -2.50)
  score 28d after the offence   100   (penalty +0.00)
[reward cap] rewards saturate at +20 over the rolling window
  5 carpools (+25 raw)                       120
[floor + clock skew] hard floor at 0; future events clamp
  40 fresh offences (-200 raw)               0
  offence dated 10d in the future            95

The penalty shrinks 1.25 per week (−5.00 → 0.00) — decay is genuinely linear; the integer score is that value rounded half-up, so equal changes round predictably. Rewards saturate at +20, the score floors at 0, and a future-dated event counts at full weight, never more.

1,200 / 1,200 share an admin-priority bracket where legacy tie-broke at random.

group is the analyst's complaint archetype, not the score tier. Same engine, two Configs: under the defaults the 7-day no-show window hides a 3-month history, so every group sits near base 100. A long-memory profile (90-day windows) makes history count — abuse groups M (no-shows) and X (offences) sink, while the high-demand underserved A, B, C stay near base. min and %<80 are the tail behind the mean: A stays wholly in Silver (min 81, 0%), while M hides a real penalised tail (min 39, 16% below Silver). That defaults-vs-long-memory difference is the case for tenant-configurable windows.

Walkthrough · 5 / 5 · the AI stretch
A calibrated no-show model — sized into overbooking, kept out of the score.
newton — predict (example data)
$ python -m analysis.predict          # pure-Python, zero deps · trimmed
trained on 793 users · label = unused/requests  (excluded from features)

fit:   MAE 0.030  vs  baseline 0.083    → 64% lower error
       mean predicted no-show 8.2%  (empirical 8.2%)  → calibrated

learned weights (standardized, |largest| first):
  approval_rate_pct    +1.20   ↑ more no-shows
  rejection_rate_pct   -0.12   ↓ fewer no-shows
  rejected_3mo         +0.09   ↑ more no-shows
  requests_3mo         +0.05   ↑ more no-shows
  nudge_offences_3mo   +0.03   ↑ more no-shows

use it:  attendance 92%  →  overbooking 1.09×
         fill 80 seats   →  admit 87 requests   # ranking still decides WHO

The numbers above are the case in miniature: predictions that are calibrated (8.2% predicted ≈ 8.2% observed) and beat the baseline can size overbooking safely. And the model stays out of the score — it sizes how many get a space, never who — so the ranking and every rejection read exactly as before. Capacity is learned; fairness is not.
